# A weekly BTC allocation target for drawdown management

The completed weekly rule traded less often than the daily rule but had a slightly deeper maximum drawdown. Would rebalancing to 50% BTC during positive-momentum weeks reduce the worst decline beyond what an investor could achieve by simply starting with less capital at risk? The 50% target is a prespecified equal split. This proposal has no run or measured outcome. [Weekly study](../../reports/btc-weekly-cadence-v1/README.md)

**One allocation rule.** Start with $10,000 in cash. At each completed Sunday UTC close, apply the original 20-session signal. At the first eligible Monday minute open at or after 00:05 UTC, target 50% BTC by post-cost equity if the signal is strictly positive, or zero otherwise. Rebalance even on consecutive positive signals if the weight has drifted. Between Mondays hold units and cash unchanged. BTC can exceed 50% after a price rise. Carry balances across years, use zero-interest cash and mark the last close without liquidating.

**Comparison.** Retain the daily, full-size weekly and annually updated passive BTC/cash accounts. The passive allocation uses training observations available through the preceding December and then drifts for the year. For a sizing control, start $5,000 in the original weekly account and $5,000 in cash, with no transfers. Under the frozen proportional costs, its total value is $5,000 plus half the original $10,000 weekly account’s value. This matches the first positive-signal allocation and allows later exposure to diverge. No additional training-fitted passive weight is needed.

**Test.** The primary contrast is base-cost maximum drawdown of the sizing control minus that of the weekly-target account, measured as positive loss fractions. A positive difference favors rebalancing. Trimming appreciated BTC may soften a later reversal. Replenishing BTC after a fall may worsen a continuing decline. Record how much was traded during unchanged signals, BTC weights around each major decline, costs, terminal wealth and net Sharpe. A lower drawdown only against the full-size account would leave simple sizing as a sufficient explanation. Proportionally scaling returns at a zero cash rate leaves Sharpe unchanged.

Freeze these choices before a separately authorized evaluation. Report gross, base- and double-cost outcomes, pooled and by calendar year. For the primary contrast, rebuild both daily wealth paths from the same within-year block draws under the existing bootstrap settings and report a paired interval. Drawdown depends on the order of returns and a few extremes. An interval spanning zero leaves the incremental risk benefit uncertain. Any replay on 2020–2024 remains retrospective.
