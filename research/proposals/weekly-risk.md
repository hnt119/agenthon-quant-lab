# A weekly BTC allocation target for drawdown management

Would rebalancing to a 50% BTC target during positive-momentum weeks reduce the worst decline beyond what an investor could achieve by starting with less capital at risk? The completed weekly study had fewer fills and a slightly deeper drawdown than daily momentum. This proposed comparison has no run or measured outcome. A replay on the previously examined 2020–2024 history would provide retrospective evidence about sizing and rebalancing. [Weekly study](../../reports/btc-weekly-cadence-v1/README.md)

## Allocation and comparators

Start with $10,000 in cash. At each completed Sunday UTC close, apply the original 20-session signal. At the first eligible trade-bearing Monday minute open at or after 00:05 UTC, target 50% BTC by post-cost equity for a strictly positive signal and zero otherwise. Measure BTC at the unadjusted execution quote when solving for the post-cost weight. Charge the same fees and adverse slippage on each fill, including rebalances on consecutive positive signals. Hold the resulting units and zero-interest cash between Mondays. The BTC weight can drift above or below 50%. Carry balances through year ends and mark the final close without liquidating.

Retain the original daily momentum, full-size weekly momentum and annually updated passive BTC/cash accounts. The passive rule sets its annual allocation from training observations available through the previous December and then lets the weight drift. Add one sizing control with $5,000 in the original weekly rule and $5,000 held in inactive cash, with no transfers. This control and the target start with the same total capital. On the first purchase at positive costs the active sleeve pays its own fees and slippage. Its BTC, marked at the unadjusted quote, is worth less than the $5,000 reserve, so BTC represents **slightly less than 50%** of total post-cost wealth. At zero costs the two initial weights coincide. Record both first-fill weights and their difference at each cost setting.

## Wealth, Sharpe and drawdown

Let `W[t]` be the close wealth of the original $10,000 weekly account for the same cost setting. With fractional units and proportional costs the control's total wealth is `S[t] = 5000 + 0.5 * W[t]`. Its daily return is the full-size weekly return multiplied by `0.5 * W[t-1] / S[t-1]`. That multiplier changes with the active sleeve's wealth. Compute the control's net Sharpe directly from its own daily total-wealth returns using the original zero cash rate, daily sample standard deviation and 365-day annualization. Report an undefined Sharpe if the standard deviation is zero.

Measure daily close drawdown as `1 - total wealth / running peak`, including inactive cash. The pooled peak starts at initial total wealth and carries across years. For each calendar-year statistic, seed a fresh peak with the preceding year-end closing wealth (initial capital for 2020) and then update it with that year's closes. Only the peak used for the annual statistic restarts. Holdings, cash and the pooled peak carry forward.

The primary contrast is base-cost maximum drawdown of the sizing control minus that of the weekly-target account, expressed in percentage points. A positive value favors rebalancing. Report gross, base- and double-cost outcomes, pooled and by year. Include turnover during unchanged signals, exposure around declines, costs, terminal wealth and net Sharpe. Trimming after a rally could soften a reversal, while replenishing BTC during a fall could deepen losses. An improvement relative only to the full-size weekly account would leave initial sizing as a sufficient explanation.

## Paired uncertainty

Use the [weekly study's bootstrap settings](../../experiments/btc-weekly-cadence-v1/protocol.json): 4,000 draws, seed 20261121, circular blocks of 20 sessions within each calendar year. For each replicate, sample the same dated indices for the **full-size weekly** and **weekly-target** daily net returns. Truncate the last block of each year to its observed length and concatenate years in their original order. Compound both sampled return paths from $10,000 without resetting capital at year boundaries. At every simulated close construct the sizing control as `5000 + 0.5 * resampled full-size weekly wealth`. This keeps the same $5,000 reserve throughout every path. Directly resampling the historical control returns would lose that identity.

Compute both pooled drawdowns on each replicate, take their paired difference in percentage points, and report its 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles. The interval is conditional on the observed return paths and folds. It does not replay trading on synthetic prices or refit the signal. Drawdown depends on the order of returns and on a few extreme observations. An interval spanning zero leaves the incremental risk benefit uncertain. Freeze these choices before any separately authorized evaluation.
